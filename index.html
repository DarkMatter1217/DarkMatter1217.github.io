Background & Philosophy

I’m Prabhjot Singh, an MSc Computer Science student in the Applied AI concentration at the University of Ottawa. Before that I did a BTech in Computer Software Engineering at Delhi Technological University.

Most of my work is on LLM and RAG systems, and on one question in particular: is the answer actually true? My system ARA checks RAG answers claim by claim against independent web evidence, and my paper on it was published in 2026. Along the way I’ve interned on legal-AI retrieval, semantic search APIs and customer segmentation.

“An AI answer should come with its evidence. If the evidence isn’t there, the system should say so instead of guessing.”
  1. 01

    Claims, not paragraphs

    Break every answer into small, self-contained claims so each one can be checked on its own.

  2. 02

    Independent evidence

    Look for support across several reliable sources instead of trusting whatever was retrieved.

  3. 03

    Honest uncertainty

    When the evidence isn’t there, answer INSUFFICIENT rather than guess.

Published · 2026 Amity Journal of Computational Sciences · Vol. 10, Issue 1

ARA — Claim-Level Verification for RAG

RAG systems usually trust whatever they retrieve. ARA splits an answer into separate claims, searches the web for independent evidence on each one, and labels it SUPPORTED, CONTRADICTED or INSUFFICIENT. If it can’t find support, it says so instead of guessing.
